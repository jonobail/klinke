# PS-3100

Fully polyphonic synthesizer (1977): 48 keys, each with its own filter, amplifier and envelope.
Sources: a photo of the front panel (greatsynthesizers.com), the *PS-3100 / PS-3300 Resonators*
schematic (K. Ishii, 23–24 Feb. 1977) and the *PS-3100 board contacts* list (synthfool.com).

Code: `core/ps3100.ts` (maths, tested in `tests/ps3100.test.ts`), `audio/ps3100-unit.ts`, catalog
entry `ps3100` in `core/gear.ts`, and its own panel `gear/ps3100-panel.*`, laid out like the
photo: TEMPERAMENT ADJUST down the left; SIGNAL GENERATORS, DYNAMIC LP FILTERS, ENVELOPE
MODIFIERS, RESONATORS with SAMPLE & HOLD; TOTAL SIGNAL MODIFIERS, the volumes and POWER along the
top right; MODULATION GENERATOR 1 / 2, GENERAL ENVELOPE GENERATOR, VOLTAGE PROCESSORS and the
patch panel below; the 48 keys in the rosewood case with the pitch lever to their left.

## Modelled

- **Every key its own voice** (the KLM-69 boards carry Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release, Hold and
  a filter and VCA per key channel): any number of notes at once. A voice is built when its key
  goes down and freed when its release has died away.
- **Signal generators:** WAVE FORM SAW / TRI / PULSE 50 % / 30 % / 10 % / PWM (the six selects on
  KLM-63); PWM swings the pulse with MG1 by the PULSE WIDTH MODULATION INTENSITY; FREQUENCY FINE
  (±50 cent) and COARSE (±a fifth); SCALE 16' / 8' / 4' (the divider outputs on KLM-64);
  FREQUENCY MODULATION from MG1 and from the S/H, up to an octave.
- **TEMPERAMENT ADJUST:** one trimmer per note name, ±50 cent, applied to every octave (the
  hardware tunes the twelve top-octave notes that everything divides down from).
- **Dynamic LP filters:** CUTOFF FREQUENCY 30 Hz–18 kHz, PEAK, key follow per voice, KBD FILTER
  BALANCE (±2 octaves tilt between the low and high ends: the FC U / FC L voltages), EXPAND (the
  key's envelope into its filter), CUT OFF FREQUENCY MODULATION from MG1 and the S/H.
- **Envelope modifiers:** ATTACK 2 ms–10 s, DECAY 4 ms–10 s, SUSTAIN; RELEASE DAMPED (keys stop
  short) or ON (they die away at the decay time); KBD HOLD keeps keys sounding until it's off.
- **Resonators** (schematic): a 2-pole input high-pass at ≈58 Hz (2 × 0.033 µF, 100 kΩ / 68 kΩ),
  then three parallel band-passes with Q = 5 (each pairs 0.082 µF with 820 pF: √(C1/C2) / 2; the
  other colour rows of the table keep the same ratio), summed. RESONANCE INTENSITY crossfades
  from the dry signal (RES OUT 2) to the peaks (RES OUT 1), as the 2.5 kΩ blend pot does.
  RESONATOR 1–3 set the peaks, 60 Hz–6 kHz; PEAK FREQUENCY MODULATION BY MG 2 sweeps all three
  together (one exponential converter drives the three photocoupler LEDs).
- **Total signal modifiers:** AMPLITUDE MODULATOR (MG2 tremolo depth), ENSEMBLE (three short
  delays swept a third of a cycle apart, slow plus fast, mixed with the dry signal), VCA 1, VCA 2
  under the general envelope when its orange switch is on, KEYBOARD VOLUME BALANCE (louder at
  one end), FINAL VOLUME.
- **Modulation:** MG1 saw down / saw up / triangle / rectangle / pink / white noise (the KLM-63
  outputs), 0.05–40 Hz; MG2 sine, 0.05–40 Hz; SAMPLE & HOLD of white noise, CLOCK FREQUENCY
  0.2–40 Hz or SYNCHRO to MG1. The MG LEDs blink at their rate.
- **General envelope generator:** DELAY, ATTACK, RELEASE; KBD TRIGGER SELECT OFF / SINGLE (from
  silence) / MULTI (every key); AUTO fires it by itself on MG2's clock.
- **Outputs:** FINAL OUT (FINAL VOLUME), PHONES (PHONE VOLUME) and DIRECT OUT (DIRECT VOLUME:
  after the resonators, before the total signal modifiers).
- **Pitch lever:** ±2 semitones, springs back.

## Interpretation

- **The patch panel isn't patchable here.** KLINKE's cables carry audio, so only the three audio
  outputs are jacks; the control-voltage jacks are drawn for the look. What would normally be
  patched is wired in: the second intensity knob of each modulation strip takes the S/H; the
  EXTERNAL PEAK FREQUENCY MODULATION knob takes the general envelope; VOLTAGE PROCESSOR 1 sends the
  general envelope to the cutoff and VP2 to the pitch, LIMITER A pushing up and LIMITER B down.
- The divider bank keeps every key phase-locked to one master oscillator; here each voice has
  its own oscillator, started when its key goes down.
- The WAVE FORM order and the RELEASE switch's two positions are read from the photo and the
  connector list; ranges not printed anywhere (times, rates, depths) are chosen to sound and
  behave like the instrument.
