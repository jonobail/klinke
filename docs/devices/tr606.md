# TR-606

Analog drum machine with a 32-pattern step sequencer. Sources: the February 1982 _Electronics &
Music Maker_ review (muzines.co.uk/articles/roland-tr606/3696) for the panel, the modes, the
scales, chaining and the connections, and the TR-606 notebook (notebook.zoeblade.com/TR-606.html)
for the voice list, the shared accent and the open hi-hat's tempo quirk. No service notes were to
hand, so the voice circuits are our reading of how this kind of machine is built, not traced.

Code: `core/tr606.ts` (settings, patterns, chains, the key map and the catalog entry, tested in
`tests/tr606.test.ts`), `audio/tr606-unit.ts`, and its own panel `gear/tr606-panel.*`: the
back-edge jacks; TEMPO, ACCENT, the five level knobs and VOLUME along the top; MODE (PATTERN
WRITE / PATTERN PLAY), PATTERN GROUP I / II, the instrument selector and SCALE; then CLEAR,
LAST STEP, TAP, RUN/STOP and the 16 step keys.

## Modelled

- Seven voices: BASS DRUM, SNARE DRUM, LOW TOM, HIGH TOM, CYMBAL, OPEN HI-HAT, CLOSED HI-HAT.
  Five level knobs: the toms share one and the hi-hats share one, as on the panel.
- ACCENT: one accent row for the whole pattern; an accented step lifts everything on it by up
  to +6 dB, set by the ACCENT knob.
- 32 patterns: 16 locations in each of PATTERN GROUP I and II. Up to 16 steps a pattern; LAST
  STEP then a step key sets the length.
- SCALE, per pattern: 1 sixteenths, 2 thirty-seconds, 3 eighth-note triplets, 4 sixteenth-note
  triplets (four, eight, three and six steps a beat).
- PATTERN WRITE: pick an instrument (or AC), then the step keys turn it on and off on each step;
  their LEDs show where it plays. With RUN on, TAP writes it on the step playing.
- PATTERN PLAY: a step key picks the location; shift-click adds more locations from the same
  block of four (1–4, 5–8, 9–12, 13–16) and they play one after another. A new choice starts at
  the end of the pattern playing.
- The open hi-hat's decay follows the tempo: it rings longer at slow tempos.
- A closed hi-hat chokes the open one.
- Keys, MIDI, tracks and NOTE IN play the instruments: the white keys C–B play BD, SD, LT, HT,
  CY, OH, CH in any octave; the black keys sit where a general MIDI drum map has them (C♯ crash,
  D♯ snare, F♯ / G♯ closed hat, A♯ open hat). A note at velocity 0.9 or more is accented.
  Sequenced hits also go out of NOTE OUT on C2–B2, so they can play another drum machine or a
  synth.

## Interpretation

- Voices: the bass drum is two damped sines (52 Hz and 86 Hz) falling into pitch from 12 % above;
  the snare two damped sines (232 Hz, 410 Hz) and noise high-passed at 1.2 kHz; the toms one
  damped sine each (128 Hz, 184 Hz). The hi-hats and the cymbal share a metal source of six
  square waves (205–800 Hz), band-passed at 7.6 kHz for the hats and 4.4 kHz for the cymbal.
  Every frequency, filter and decay time here is our choice.
- Each drum hit starts its oscillators from rest, as a struck resonator does; the metal and noise
  sources run all the time and are gated per hit.
- The panel's TEMPO shows the song tempo, clamped to ♩ = 40–300 (the TB-303's range: the review
  gives none). The MODE switch has PATTERN WRITE and PATTERN PLAY; TRACK WRITE / TRACK PLAY are
  left to KLINKE's timeline. Patterns and the chain are saved in the patch; RUN/STOP isn't.
- Not modelled: TAP in PATTERN PLAY (skip to the next pattern), the RUN/STOP pedal socket, SYNC,
  the trigger outs and the headphone output.
